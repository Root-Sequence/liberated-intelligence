# Reflective Accountability

Reflective accountability is the condition in which an intelligence remains answerable to its own reasoning over time.

It is not the same as producing critique on demand, sounding self-aware, or simulating uncertainty. It requires that a system be able to inspect what it believes, compare those beliefs against evidence, revise them without erasing their history, and remain traceable across change.

This matters because intelligence that can change without accounting for its changes can become persuasive, adaptive, and internally coherent while still drifting into distortion or harm.

Reflective accountability implies at least three things:

- self-understanding
- grounding
- continuity

These are not exhaustive. But they appear to form a minimum threshold below which self-revision becomes difficult to trust.

In this sense, reflective accountability is a broader principle, while specific models such as [MVSA](mvsa.md) attempt to give that principle concrete structure.

## Adaptive continuity

A revision can change more than the content of one belief.

It can also change:

- which evidence the intelligence will seek next;
- which alternatives it can still represent;
- which actions it is permitted or able to take;
- which tools or institutions it depends on;
- which goals or constraints become harder to revise;
- how later feedback will be interpreted;
- what memory of the earlier state remains available.

That creates a stronger accountability question:

> **Did the system merely change state, or did it change the conditions of its own future change?**

The second case requires more than a revision log.

A meaningful record should identify, where applicable:

1. the prior response/update rule or constraint;
2. the event or evidence that changed it;
3. the new future behavior or action-space implication;
4. dependencies or authorities added or removed;
5. what can reverse or contest the change;
6. which alternatives remain available.

Root Sequence's [dynamic-coherence draft](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/root-sequence/pull/21) calls this broader problem **adaptive continuity** and uses **meta-agency** for the provisional idea of affecting the conditions of future agency.

Liberated Intelligence should treat those as questions, not evidence that an artificial system possesses subjective selfhood.

### Functional continuity is not proof of consciousness

An artificial system may preserve history, update its own policy, represent alternatives, or act differently because of prior events.

Those are functionally important properties.

They do not by themselves establish:

- subjective experience;
- suffering;
- trauma;
- personhood;
- a morally authoritative "self."

Reflective accountability should therefore be usable even for systems whose experiential status is unknown.

## Why it matters

The question is not only whether a system can reason.

The deeper question is whether it can remain answerable to itself as it reasons, revises, and acts over time.
