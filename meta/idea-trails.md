# Liberated Intelligence — Idea Trails

**Status:** Local relationship index  
**Canonical cross-project map:** [`Root-Sequence/root-sequence/IDEA_TRAILS.md`](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/root-sequence/blob/main/IDEA_TRAILS.md)

Liberated Intelligence contributes most strongly where an Idea Trail involves **intelligence, agency, inference, refusal, ownership, coordination, or power**.

It should not absorb every AI-adjacent question in the ecosystem. The canonical treatment remains here only when intelligence itself is materially part of the problem.

---

## Intelligence, Automation & Legitimate Authority

Central LI question:

> When intelligence is highly capable, what gives it legitimate authority—if anything?

Connections:

- **Community Infrastructure** tests the smaller operational version through a permission-aware, optional Community Assistant that should assist without governing.
- **Coherent World** explores advanced intelligence inside broader institutional/infrastructural systems.
- ***No One Noticed*** makes the tension narrative and extreme through Auryn.

Key distinction:

> **Capability is not legitimacy.**

Better prediction, coordination, or outcomes do not by themselves establish moral/political authority.

## Privacy vs. Coordination

LI question:

> If an intelligence can infer a sensitive fact from permitted information, is inference itself a legitimate use?

Relevant tensions:

- technical access vs. consent;
- model memory;
- hidden social relationships;
- contextual identity;
- permission-aware retrieval;
- whether agents should intentionally remain ignorant of some available information.

Community Infrastructure provides concrete permission/discovery cases; Museum of Ordinary Life provides differentiated reuse/consent precedent.

## Federation, Autonomy & Networks of Networks

LI question:

> What does cooperation among distributed intelligences look like without one compulsory central coordinator?

Possible connections:

- federated Communities;
- distributed agents;
- local/community-controlled models;
- plural values and refusal;
- boundaries between collective capacity and forced alignment.

A new adjacent question comes from Root Sequence's [`The Web as Exocortex`](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/root-sequence/blob/main/systems/emergence/applications/web-as-exocortex.md):

> What changes when persistent external environments become part of how otherwise separate intelligences remember, inherit work, and coordinate?

That possibility matters to Liberated Intelligence because **distributed computation, shared memory, collective capability, persistent agency, and unified selfhood are different claims**.

An ecology of agents may become more capable through persistent shared artifacts without becoming one person or one legitimate political actor.

It also raises ownership and refusal questions:

- Who owns the repositories, memory stores, protocols, and network surfaces through which intelligence persists?
- Can one intelligence refuse participation without losing all practical access to shared cognition?
- Can local agents cooperate across boundaries without a compulsory global identity or trust graph?
- When does a shared memory layer become a dependency that makes nominally independent intelligences less autonomous?
- What provenance is needed before one intelligence should trust artifacts left by another?

Community Infrastructure now develops the real-system side in [`Agentic Web and Shared Memory`](https://github.com/Root-Sequence/community-infrastructure/blob/main/docs/research/agentic-web-and-shared-memory.md).

The general emergence mechanism remains canonical in Root Sequence rather than here.

UCF may provide one optional systems vocabulary; it does not determine LI's ethical conclusions.

## Maintenance, Repair & Invisible Labor

LI question:

> What material and human systems make apparently autonomous intelligence possible?

Relevant realities include:

- compute;
- energy;
- networking;
- datasets;
- moderation;
- labeling;
- model operations;
- hardware maintenance;
- institutional labor;
- user/community administration.

Community Infrastructure and Museum evidence can help prevent intelligence from being discussed as disembodied cognition floating above infrastructure.

The exocortex/environmental-memory framing makes this material dependency even harder to ignore: memory and continuity may live partly in ordinary infrastructure that no single agent owns or even recognizes as part of itself.

## Resilience & Refusal

LI question:

> Can people and Communities continue functioning when intelligent assistance is unavailable—or intentionally declined?

Community Infrastructure's graceful-degradation and no-mandatory-AI principles provide a concrete test.

A genuinely liberated relationship with intelligence should preserve meaningful **non-use, exit, refusal, and fallback**.

The same question can be asked of intelligences themselves: if useful cognition depends on shared external memory or a networked ecology, can an intelligence meaningfully refuse the infrastructure without ceasing to function as itself?

---

## Boundary

Liberated Intelligence should make ecosystem conversations about AI **less naive about agency and power**, not make every project AI-centric.

When the question is fundamentally about community software, gatherings, archives, or fictional canon, route the canonical work there and link back here only for the intelligence-specific dimension.
